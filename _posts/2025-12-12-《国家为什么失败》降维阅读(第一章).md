# Ch.1 SO CLOSE AND YET SO DIFFERENT

## THE ECONOMICS OF THE RIO GRANDE 

The city of Nogales is cut in half by a fence. If you stand by the fence and look north, you will see Nogales, Arizona.

The income of the average household there is about $30,000 a year. Most teenagers are in school, and the majority of adults are high school graduates.

Some people argue that the U.S. health care system is **deficient**. They complain that it is missing necessary parts or is not good enough. Despite these arguments, the population is relatively healthy. They have high **life expectancy**, meaning most people live for a very long time.

Many residents are old, but they **have access to** Medicare. This means they are able to get and use this government service to pay for doctors.

It is just one of the many services the government provides. Most people **take for granted** these services. They expect electricity and telephones to always be there, so they use them without thinking or feeling grateful.

They also have a **sewage system**. This system uses pipes to carry dirty water and waste away from their toilets and sinks. There is a road network linking them to other cities. And, **last but not least**, there is **law and order**. This point comes at the end, but it is very important: the police enforce the rules and keep the streets safe.

The people of Nogales, Arizona, can **go about** their daily activities. They can simply do their normal work and shopping without fear. They are not afraid of theft. They are not afraid of **expropriation**. This happens when the government forcibly takes away your private property.

They do not worry about things that might **jeopardize** their investments. They know their businesses and houses are safe; nothing will put them in danger or risk.

Equally important, the residents **take it for granted** (believe without question) that the government is their **agent**. An **agent** is someone who acts for you and serves you.

Of course, there is sometimes inefficiency and **corruption**. **Corruption** happens when officials are dishonest or take illegal money. But generally, the people control the government. They can vote to replace their leaders.

**Democracy** is **second nature** to them. Like walking or breathing, it is a habit that feels automatic and natural.

Life south of the fence, just a few feet away, is rather different.

The residents of Nogales, Sonora, live in a relatively **prosperous** part of Mexico. Compared to other poor parts of the country, this area has more money and business success. However, the income of the average household there is about one-third of the income in Nogales, Arizona.

Most adults in Nogales, Sonora, do not have a high school degree. Many teenagers are not in school.

Mothers have to worry about high rates of **infant mortality**. They are constantly afraid that their babies might die before they reach one year old. Public health conditions are poor. So, it is no surprise that residents do not live as long as their northern neighbors.

They also do not **have access to** many services. They cannot reach or use necessary things easily. Specifically, they lack **public amenities**. For example, they do not have nice parks, libraries, or clean public restrooms that make life pleasant. Roads are in bad condition south of the fence.

**Law and order** is in worse condition. The police do not enforce the rules well, so the streets are not safe. Crime is high.

Opening a business is a **risky** activity. It is dangerous, and there is a high chance you will lose your investment. You risk robbery.

Also, you face the problem of **greasing all the palms**. If you want to get work done, you must secretly put money into the hands of officials to bribe them. Just to open a business is no easy **endeavor**. It is a very difficult attempt or effort that takes a lot of energy.

Residents of Nogales, Sonora, live with politicians’ **corruption**. They deal with leaders who are dishonest and steal public money. They also suffer from the politicians’ **ineptitude**. The leaders are simply not good at their jobs; they have no skill and make bad decisions.

**In contrast to** their northern neighbors, the situation here is opposite. Democracy is a very recent experience for them.

Until the political reforms of 2000, the country was under the control of the **Institutional** Revolutionary Party (PRI). This was a large, organized political group that held power for a long time.

How could the two halves of what is **essentially** the same city be so different? **Essentially** means fundamentally or at the most basic level; deep down, they are one city.

There is no difference in geography or climate. There is no difference in the types of diseases **prevalent** in the area. These diseases are common and found everywhere in this region. This is since germs do not face any **restrictions**; no rules or walls stop them from crossing back and forth between the United States and Mexico.

Of course, health conditions are very different. But this has nothing to do with the **disease environment**. It is not because the local germs are stronger.

It is because the people south of the border live with **inferior** **sanitary** conditions. **Inferior** means lower in quality or worse than others. **Sanitary** conditions relate to health and cleanliness, like clean water and trash removal. The people also lack **decent** health care. The care is not good enough or acceptable for a comfortable life.

But perhaps the residents are very different. Could it be that the residents of Nogales, Arizona, are grandchildren of **migrants** from Europe? **Migrants** are people who move from one place to live in another. Could it be that those in the south are **descendants** of Aztecs? **Descendants** are the children, grandchildren, and future generations of a specific group.

Not so. The backgrounds of people on both sides of the border are **quite** similar.

After Mexico became independent from Spain in 1821, the area around “Los dos Nogales” was part of the Mexican state of Vieja California. It remained so even after the Mexican-American War of 1846–1848.

Indeed, it was only after the **Gadsden Purchase** of 1853 that the U.S. border was **extended** into this area. To **extend** means to make something cover a larger area; the US map grew bigger to include this land.

It was Lieutenant N. Michler who, while **surveying** the border, noted something. He was examining and measuring the land to make a map. He noted the presence of the “pretty little valley of Los Nogales.”

Here, on either side of the border, the two cities **rose up**.

The **inhabitants** of Nogales, Arizona, and Nogales, Sonora, share ancestors. **Inhabitants** are the people who live in a specific place. They enjoy the same food and the same music.

And, we would **hazard** to say, they have the same “culture.” To **hazard** means to risk saying something that might be wrong, but here we are confident enough to make that guess.

Of course, there is a very simple and obvious explanation for the differences between the two halves of Nogales. You have probably **long since** guessed it. You likely figured it out a long time ago. It is the very border that defines the two halves.

Nogales, Arizona, is in the United States. Its **inhabitants** have access to the **economic institutions** of the United States. **Institutions** are not just buildings; they are the rules, laws, and systems that organize how a society works.

These institutions **enable** the people to choose their occupations freely. The rules make it possible for them to pick any job they want. They can **acquire** schooling and skills. They can get and gain this knowledge to improve themselves.

The institutions encourage employers to invest in the best technology. This leads to higher wages for the workers.

They also have access to **political institutions**. These systems allow them to **take part in** the democratic process. They participate by voting. They can **elect** their representatives. If the politicians **misbehave** (act badly or dishonestly), the people can replace them.

**In consequence**, politicians provide the basic services. As a direct result of this pressure from voters, the government does its job. These services **range from** public health **to** roads **to** law and order.

Those of Nogales, Sonora, are not so lucky. They live in a different world shaped by different institutions.

These different institutions create very **disparate** **incentives**. **Disparate** means the situations are completely different and not equal at all. **Incentives** are the reasons or rewards that motivate you to do something. For example, if you know you can keep your money, you have an **incentive** to work hard.

This affects the inhabitants and the **entrepreneurs**. **Entrepreneurs** are the brave people who start new businesses and take risks to make money. The bad **incentives** stop them from investing.

These **incentives**, created by the institutions of the countries in which the cities are **situated** (located), are the main reason for the differences. They explain the difference in economic **prosperity** (wealth and success).

Why are the institutions of the United States so much more **conducive to** economic success? Why are they so helpful and favorable for creating wealth?

Or, **for that matter**, why are they better than the rest of Latin America? When we say "**for that matter**," we mean we are also including this related topic in our question.

The answer to this question lies in the way the different societies formed during the early **colonial period**. This was the time when European nations took control of these lands.

An institutional **divergence** took place then. A **divergence** happens when one road splits into two different paths, and they go in separate directions.

This separation has **implications** lasting into the present day. The **implications** are the strong effects and consequences that we still feel today. To understand this **divergence**, we must begin right at the **foundation** of the colonies in North and Latin America.

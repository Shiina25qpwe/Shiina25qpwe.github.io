# Reading Passage 1: GayGene

Genetics and Sexuality (Re-written for Contextual Learning)

**Part 1: The Search for a Cause**

Biology helps decide our **sexual orientation**. It determines the direction of our romantic interest, whether we like men, women, or both. In 1993, scientists found a region of the human **genome** (the complete set of our genes) called Xq28. They said it was **linked to** male homosexuality. It was connected or related to being gay. Because of this, the **controversial notion** of a “gay gene” was born. This idea caused a lot of public argument and disagreement.

However, those research findings have not been **replicated**. Other scientists tried to do the experiment again to get the same result, but they failed. It was never going to be simple. Decades of research show that almost every human **characteristic**, such as height or eye color, is complicated. It is a **complex interplay** of different things. It is a mix of genes and the world around us acting together. A new study **confirms** that this is true. It proves and makes it certain that this rule applies to human sexuality, too.

**Part 2: How the Study Was Done**

This study was the largest ever on this difficult topic. It was **conducted by** an international group of scientists. They led and performed the work together with a company called 23andMe. This company focuses on personal **genomics**. They study the structure and function of genes for individuals.

The scientists used a method called a **genome-wide association study** (GWAS). They looked at data from over 470,000 people. All these people remained **anonymous**. Their names were hidden so no one knew who they were. They also **consented to** the study. They said "yes" and agreed to let the scientists use their data.

**Part 3: Finding the Connections**

A GWAS involves **scanning** a person’s DNA. Computers look closely and quickly at the code to find tiny **variations**. These are small changes or differences in the genetic letters (A, T, G, or C). Scientists look for changes that **correlate with** a specific feature. If the gene changes, the feature changes too; they have a relationship.

The **participants** (the people taking part in the study) were divided into groups. They were divided **on the basis of** their answers. The scientists used their answers as the reason or foundation for the groups. They asked: “Have you ever had sex with someone of the same sex?”

However, this question is a **woolly proxy** for true **sexual orientation**. It is a fuzzy, unclear substitute, not a perfect measure of whether someone is actually gay. This is true even **in the absence of** lies. Even without people telling **white lies**—those small, harmless untruths people tell to be polite or hide things—the data is not perfect. Therefore, the figures relate only to a single act, not to how someone **identifies as** gay. It does not reflect how they see or label themselves.

**Part 4: The Results**

The researchers found five **genetic markers**. These are specific flags or points in the DNA. These markers were **associated with** same-sex behavior. They were connected to it. None of these markers was on the sex chromosomes (X or Y).

Surprisingly, their total effect **accounted for** a very small part of the difference. These genes explained or were the reason for less than 1% of the **variance**. They did not cause the whole range of differences between people. This is because the behavior is the result of the **aggregate effort** of many genes. It comes from the total sum or combined power of hundreds of genes working together. Their individual effects are **infinitesimally weak**. Each one is so incredibly small and tiny that it is hard to measure.

**Part 5: Differences Between Sexes**

The scientists tried **totting up** all the thousands of genetic variants. They added them all together to get a sum. Together, they **accounted for** between 8% and 25% of the variation in behavior. These variants also **overlapped with** other traits. They shared common ground with things like smoking and an **openness** to new experiences (being willing to try new things).

Interestingly, only about 60% of the variants were shared by both men and women. This fact is **intimating** a difference. It suggests or hints that being gay might be different for men and women. David Curtis from University College London notes this. He says there might be specific factors **affecting** same-sex attraction. These factors influence or change the result. It is not just about **attraction** to males **versus** females. It is not simply a choice of one against the other.

**Part 6: Future Challenges**

The **riddles** go on. These puzzles and mysteries continue. The scientists were **conscious of** the tricky subject matter. They were fully aware and kept it in mind. They were **at pains to** avoid trouble. They tried very hard and made a great effort to **anticipate** problems. They wanted to predict misunderstandings before they happened. They wanted to avoid a **backlash**. They did not want a strong, angry reaction from the public. To help with this, they **collaborated with** LGBT groups. They worked together with these **advocacy groups**, organizations that support and speak for gay rights.

Yet, the research only **scrapes the surface**. It only looks at the top layer and does not go deep into the mysterious depths of human sexuality. **Unravelling** these riddles will be difficult. Untying, solving, and explaining these mysteries takes time. It will **inevitably** cause trouble. It is certain to happen and cannot be avoided. It will **beget** confusion. It will produce or give birth to **misconception** (wrong ideas) and controversy.

But at least this study helps. It should **add weight to** a specific view. It gives more importance and support to the idea that non-heterosexual behavior is normal. It is firmly within the natural **spectrum**. It sits on the wide range or scale of human behavior. It is part of human **diversity**. It shows that humans have many different types and varieties. This study provides a firm **foundation** for future work. It is a solid base to build on.

# Reading Passage 2: Tesla

The Battle for Electric Vehicle Dominance: NIO vs. Tesla

**Part 1: The Chinese Contender**

William Bin Li is as close as China may have come to **cloning** Elon Musk. It is almost as if China tried to copy or duplicate the famous American entrepreneur. Mr. Li is the founder of NIO, a **swanky** electric-vehicle (EV) company. The cars are fancy, stylish, and expensive. He is in his 40s, a tech **nerd** who loves computers and gadgets, and even though he is not as **meme-able** as the founder of Tesla—meaning his photos aren't turned into funny internet jokes as often—he is treated like a rock star by his **adoring** customers who love and admire him greatly.

NIO is worth $4 billion. This is just a **fraction** of the size of Mr. Musk’s Tesla, which is valued at $75 billion. NIO is only a small part or piece compared to the giant Tesla. But of all China’s **30-odd** EV startups (meaning roughly 30 or so companies), it is the best known. It also raises and **dashes** investors’ hopes with **Tesla-like frequency**. Just like Tesla, NIO often lifts people's hopes up high and then suddenly smashes or destroys them very often.

On December 30th, NIO’s shares **soared** 54%. The stock price flew up high very quickly when Mr. Li said **output** (the amount of cars produced) had probably reached 8,000 vehicles in the fourth quarter. This was up from almost 4,800 in the third quarter. But over the whole of 2019, the shares lost almost 40%.

**Part 2: Ambitions and Struggles**

In truth, Mr. Musk is **in a league of his own**. He is so good or different that he has no real competition in his level. But Mr. Li has long **had the edge on** him in one respect. He has had an advantage over Musk in one specific area. As our report says, NIO is **emblematic** of China’s ambition. NIO is a symbol or a perfect example of China's goal to be a **hub** of global EV production—a central point or center of activity. China wants to dominate electric vehicles in the 21st century **as squarely as** America did the **internal-combustion engine** (the traditional gas engine) in the 20th century. They want to do it just as directly and firmly.

As such, China would be a natural place to produce a “Tesla killer,” as NIO was **dubbed**. This was the nickname given to NIO during a TV interview. Yet if NIO is trying to **topple** Tesla—if it is trying to push Tesla down from its top position—it is **going about it** in an **odd** way. It is handling the task strangely.

It is copying some of the very aspects of Tesla’s **business model** that have made the American firm’s survival a matter of constant concern. For example, it is doing **lavish** spending on **sophisticated** technology. It is spending huge, excessive amounts of money on very complex and advanced tech. This results in **correspondingly** large holes in its **cashflow statements**. The loss of money matches the high spending perfectly.

If anything can kill Tesla, it is more likely to be its **perennial** difficulty in **generating** cash. Tesla has a lasting, recurring problem with making money, which is a bigger threat than competition from a Chinese **upstart**. An upstart is a new, arrogant company that has suddenly risen to power but has the same problem. **Ironically**, it could be China that **ultimately** secures Tesla a bright future. In a twist of fate that seems opposite to what you expect, China might be the one to finally make Tesla safe.

**Part 3: Rising High, Falling Hard**

If only NIO could be so lucky. Not long ago, NIO was considered the more **promising** of the two. It seemed more likely to succeed. In the world’s largest car market, where sales of luxury vehicles are **booming** (growing very fast), it got its start in 2014. At that time, state and local governments were **throwing subsidies at** both buyers and manufacturers. They were giving away free government money to encourage people to buy and make EVs. **Venture-capital** backing was **abundant**. Money from investors who support risky new businesses was plentiful and easy to find.

NIO delivered its first commercial car, the ES8 SUV priced at above $70,000, in 2018. Shortly afterwards, it **issued** shares on the New York Stock Exchange. It sold stock to the public, both to raise money and to **heighten** its **international profile**. It wanted to increase its fame around the world. Its shareholders include Baillie Gifford, an Edinburgh-based fund manager that is the largest **institutional investor** in Tesla (a large organization that invests money).

Tu Le of Sino Auto Insights, an advisory firm, says NIO’s Tesla-killing **aspirations** **got the better of** it, though. Its strong desires and goals to beat Tesla defeated its common sense. It was **rash** to think it could quickly **take on** a firm 11 years older. It was careless and too hasty to fight against a company with huge global **brand recognition** and several models.

NIO’s revenues are **dwarfed** by the $24 billion **projected** for Tesla. NIO's income looks tiny like a dwarf compared to the predicted income for Tesla. Yet since the start of 2017, its **cumulative** losses have **overtaken** those of Tesla. Its total losses, adding up over time, have passed Tesla's.

NIO has **splashed out on** spacious stores. It spent a lot of money freely on big shops with libraries, coffee shops, and **crèches** (nurseries for babies), sometimes directly across the street from Tesla showrooms. But **unlike** Tesla, it has not invested much in factories. Instead, it is **contracting** manufacturing to JAC Motors. It is paying another company to do the building work.

Furthermore, a cut in state subsidies for EV purchases since June has hurt **investor sentiment**. The investors' feelings and attitude turned negative. This is **prompting** fears of a **funding crunch**. It is causing or triggering a fear that money will run out tightly. NIO raised $100m from Tencent, but it **burned through** even more than that. It spent the money very fast, like fire consuming paper. It now has a **net debt** of $1.3bn. Though sales rose, it **admits** it needs funding if it is to survive. It confesses that it needs cash.

**Part 4: Government Favors**

Given the **precarious** circumstances—considering the dangerous and unstable situation—the Chinese government might be expected to **throw NIO a lifeline**. You would think they would toss a rope to save the drowning company. Instead, it is Tesla that is **getting the breaks**. Tesla is the one receiving the lucky chances and special favors.

On December 30th, the first Model 3s **rolled out** from Tesla’s **Gigafactory** in Shanghai. The cars left the production line of the giant factory. They cost a **mere** $50,000 each, which is a small amount compared to others. Though work started on the **plant** (factory) less than a year ago, production is already running fast. Days before, the American firm received $1.3 billion-worth of funding from Chinese **lenders** (banks that loan money) to complete the factory.

Production in China **spares** Tesla from import **tariffs** on **finished vehicles**. Making cars locally saves Tesla from paying extra taxes on completed cars brought from outside. Its locally made cars also **qualify for** subsidies. They meet the requirements to get government money. Its shares have soared to record highs, though there are still **nagging** doubts. There are persistent, annoying worries about its ability to increase **volumes** (quantity), **margins** (profit), and cash generation.

**Perversely**, Tesla may even have **benefited from** China’s trade war with America. Strangely and contrary to what is expected, the war helped them. The government hopes to **portray** Tesla’s investment as a symbol of its **openness**. They want to show or depict the factory as proof that China is open to the world. Tesla is the first fully foreign-owned car plant in China.

NIO, **despite** being Chinese, does not offer the same **geopolitical** advantages. It doesn't help with international politics and geography relations. Without its own factories, it has less **leverage**. It has less power or influence to negotiate when asking state governments to support it. As Michael Dunne says, “NIO doesn’t have a **clear-cut** godfather in the Chinese government.” It doesn't have a definite, obvious powerful protector. It is competing with a **host of** EV startups. It fights a large number of other new companies for funding.

In this **fraught** market, fortunes could quickly reverse again. In this anxious and stress-filled market, luck could change. Some analysts say it is unlikely the government will let NIO **go bust**. They probably won't let it go bankrupt because it is a symbol of China’s technological ambitions.

**Part 5: Tesla's Advantage**

For now, though, Tesla is in **pole position**. Like a race car at the front of the starting grid, it is in the best position to win. In fact, China must already feel like home to Mr. Musk. The government’s EV ambitions give Tesla a **tailwind**. The support pushes them forward like wind from behind, something they lack in America.

Consumers love luxury-car brands; Tesla’s main competition will be with Germany’s **premium** carmakers, not Chinese ones. The high-quality, expensive German brands are the real rivals. China’s manufacturing **prowess** will help Tesla. China's great skill and ability in making things will help Tesla overcome the “production hell” it suffered back home. And China may be quicker to encourage **autonomous** driving than America. They might support self-driving cars faster.

For Mr. Musk, the main **drawback** could be that Twitter is blocked. The biggest disadvantage or negative point is that his **favoured megaphone**—his favorite tool to shout his opinions to the world—is blocked behind the "Great Firewall" of China's internet. But for Tesla, that too must be a **blessed relief**. It must be a welcome comfort to have some quiet.

## 01

Tim sat in his big, soft **armchair** and looked at the photo on the wall. In the photo, his uncle Jack stood on a huge block of blue ice in the **Arctic**. Behind him, the sky was **aquamarine**, the color of the ocean. Uncle Jack was a brave explorer, and Tim thought he was the most **admirable** person in the world.

But last week, Uncle Jack did not come back from his last trip. A big storm hit his camp, and now no one knew where he was. Some people said the camp was **abandoned**, left all alone in the snow. Tim felt very sad, but he didn’t cry. He believed his uncle was still alive.

That afternoon, the **baby-sitter**, Anna, came to stay with him. She was nice, but she was **averse** to exciting plans. So when Tim said, “We must go to the **auction** tomorrow!” she shook her head. The auction would sell Uncle Jack’s things from his house to help pay for a new search.

“Please, Anna! There might be something important there,” Tim said. He needed to look at Uncle Jack’s diary. At first Anna said “No,” but Tim didn’t stop asking. Finally, she became **amenable** and agreed to go.

At the auction house, a **bald** man with a shiny head stood up and sang a short **anthem** to start the event. Tim looked around and felt **apprehensive**; his hands were a little cold. So many people wanted to buy his uncle’s things.

A big ice **auger** was the first item. The bald man said, “This is a tool. It makes holes in thick ice.” Next to Tim, a boy with glasses whispered, “My dad uses one like that.” Tim smiled, and they began to talk. This is how Tim became **acquainted** with a new friend, Ben.

Soon, the auction man held up a small blue stone. “An **aquamarine** from the Arctic,” he said. Tim looked at the stone and remembered Uncle Jack. A tear almost fell, but Ben put a hand on his shoulder. That helped **allay** Tim’s fear a little.

Then, the man brought out Uncle Jack’s diary. Tim knew he had to get it. He made an **appeal** to the men running the auction. “Please,” he said, “I’m his nephew. That diary might tell us where he is.” But the men said no one could take it for free; they had to wait for their turn.

Ben had an idea. “My phone has a map. Maybe the diary tells about a place.” They found a note in the diary about a special fuel **additive** that made the snowmobile go faster. It was an **adjunct** to his main fuel. There was also a note: “My most important **attribute** is never giving up hope.”

Then they read the last page. It said: “Storm coming. Moving to ice cave number three. Radio battery low, **availability** of contact will be short.”

Tim’s heart jumped. An ice cave! So the camp wasn’t really **abandoned**; Uncle Jack had moved to a safe place! He called the search team right away. Now, Tim felt sure that his uncle was alive and waiting for help. And he knew that, just like Uncle Jack, he would never give up hope.

## 02
